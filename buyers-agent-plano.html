Buying a Home in Plano

Plano is one of DFW's most established cities — mature trees, strong Plano ISD schools, and major employers like Toyota Motor North America, JPMorgan Chase and the upcoming AT&T headquarters campus in the Legacy area.

Much of Plano's housing was built in the 1980s and 1990s, so the inspection matters as much as the price. We help you plan for roof, HVAC, foundation and sewer-line checks during the option period, and we negotiate repairs or credits based on what the inspector actually finds.

Plano is in Collin & Denton Counties, with access to the Dallas North Tollway, US-75, the President George Bush Turnpike and SH-121, plus DART rail in downtown Plano. We work with buyers across ZIP codes 75023, 75024, 75025, 75074, 75075 and 75093.

Frequently Asked Questions

Buyer's Agent in Plano, TX — FAQ

Do I need a buyer's agent to buy a home in Plano?

You are not required to have one, but a buyer's agent works only for you — pricing the home against recent Plano sales, negotiating repairs and credits, and managing deadlines in the Texas contract. When you buy new construction, the builder's sales consultant represents the builder, so having your own representation is especially important.

Do I have to sign an agreement before touring homes in Texas?

Yes. Since January 1, 2026, Texas law requires a written agreement with a buyer before a license holder shows residential property. The agreement states the services, term, whether it is exclusive, and the broker's compensation — which is not set by law and is fully negotiable. We walk you through it in plain English before your first showing, along with TREC's Information About Brokerage Services notice.

How much does a buyer's agent cost in Plano?

Buyer's agent compensation is negotiable and is spelled out in writing before we start. In many transactions, part or all of it is paid through seller concessions negotiated in the offer. We explain the options up front so there are no surprises at closing.

What school districts serve Plano?

Plano ISD serves most of the city. Because zoning can vary by street, we verify the assigned schools for every home before you make an offer.

What should I know about property taxes when buying in Plano?

Texas has no state income tax, so property taxes are a larger share of ownership cost. Your total rate depends on the city, county, school district and any MUD or PID in the community. After closing, file for your homestead exemption with the appraisal district to reduce your taxable value.

What is the option period in a Texas home purchase?

The option period is a negotiated number of days, purchased with an option fee, during which you can terminate the contract for any reason. It is when you complete inspections and negotiate repairs — so we schedule your inspector right away and help you decide what to ask for.
